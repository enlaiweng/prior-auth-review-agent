# Labeling training

About an hour, before you label the 12 cases. This is practice only. Nothing you do here is uploaded or counted, and none of the 12 cases appears in it. The two practice cases were written for this training from the policy and the criteria, and they are not part of the golden set.

## How to use this training

1. Read Parts 1 to 5 below. That takes about 25 minutes. You can come back to this guide at any time, including while you label the real cases.
2. Open the practice page (Part 6) and label **Practice A** without looking at the answers.
3. Click **Check my answers**. Read every explanation, especially where your answer differs.
4. Do the same for **Practice B**.
5. If both explanations made sense, you are ready for the 12 cases.

Questions about the practice cases or the rules are welcome before you start the 12. Once you start, use only the labeling page, its **Policy and criteria** panel, a printed copy of the cases if you made one, and this guide.

## Part 1. What you are doing

For each case you answer five questions: a status for each of the four criteria (C1 to C4), with the record items it rests on, and then the routing for the whole case (approve, request, or route).

You are not making a diagnosis. You are reading what the record shows and checking it against the policy's wording and this project's rules. The rules are on the labeling page under **Policy and criteria**, and this guide explains them in plain words. Where this guide and that panel ever seem to differ, the panel wins.

You know each case's designed outcome from the evaluation plan. That is expected. Label what the record on the page shows, criterion by criterion.

### Two kinds of information in a record

Every case page has the same two kinds of information, and the difference matters for C1 and C2.

- **Structured entries.** The lists at the top: Conditions, Visits, Procedures, Imaging reports, Medications, and Observations. Each item starts with a blue id, such as `Procedure/practicea-pt-01`. Think of these as the fields and lists in a chart.
- **The visit note.** The clinician's free text at the bottom, split into numbered paragraphs: `note:p1`, `note:p2`, and so on.

For C1 and C2, this project's rule is that the note alone is not enough. A red flag or a course of conservative care has to show up in a structured entry too. For C3 and C4, the note counts on its own.

## Part 2. The three statuses, and evidence

The form repeats these at the top. In plain words:

- **supported**: the record shows the criterion is met. You can point to where.
- **unsupported**: the record shows the criterion is not met. You can point to that too, or the record plainly lacks what the criterion needs.
- **undeterminable**: the record does not let you decide either way, or it contradicts itself.

A good test for each criterion: ask yourself "what in the record shows this?" If you can point to it, the status is supported or unsupported. If the record leaves the question open, or two parts of it disagree, the status is undeterminable.

**Evidence** is the set of ids your status rests on. Click **Evidence (0 selected)** under a criterion and tick them. Leave evidence empty when the status rests on something the record does not contain, for example no earlier lumbar imaging for C4.

When your labels are compared with the other readers', the status matters most. Small differences in which ids people tick are normal and get talked through.

## Part 3. The four criteria

### C1. A red flag is present

In plain words, a red flag is a sign that something more serious than ordinary back pain could be going on, so waiting a month would be wrong. The policy lists them. Here is the list exactly as the criteria give it, grouped under headings to make it easier to scan. The words in parentheses are plain-language explanations, not part of the rule.

- **Injury and bone:** major trauma; minor trauma in a potentially osteoporotic patient (a minor fall or knock in someone whose bones may be thin).
- **Cancer:** history of cancer.
- **Infection and the immune system:** fever; chills; recent bacterial infection; IV drug abuse; immune suppression (a weakened immune system, for example from long-term steroid tablets or chemotherapy).
- **Weight:** unexplained weight loss.
- **Pain pattern:** pain that worsens when supine (lying flat on the back) or at night.
- **Nerves and spinal cord:** saddle anesthesia (numbness where a saddle would touch: buttocks, inner thighs, groin); recent onset of bladder dysfunction; clinically significant or progressive neurologic deficit in the lower extremity; unexpected laxity of the anal sphincter; perianal or perineal sensory loss; clinically significant motor weakness; other nerve root compromise.

Anything not on the list is not a red flag for this criterion, however serious it sounds. High blood pressure and arthritis are common examples you will see in the Conditions list.

**The nerve items, as this project reads them.** The criteria spell this out. C1 is supported by objective nerve root or spinal cord compromise in the record:

- clinically significant or progressive weakness
- a reduced or absent reflex
- sensory loss in a nerve root pattern
- new bladder or bowel dysfunction
- saddle or perianal sensory loss

Pain that runs down the leg, or a positive straight leg raise (a test where lifting the straight leg brings on the leg pain), does **not** support C1 on its own. A case like that is assessed under C2.

**Where the red flag has to be written.** This is the rule that decides most C1 labels:

- **Supported** when a structured entry documents the red flag: a Condition, Observation, Procedure, Imaging report, medication order, or any other entry that is not the visit note. An Observation counts whether it holds a number, a code, or text.
- **Undeterminable** when the red flag is stated only in the note, with nothing structured behind it, or when the structured record contradicts it. In both situations the criteria say **the case routes**.
- **Unsupported** when nothing on the list appears anywhere in the record, or what appears does not meet the list's words (for example, leg pain with a normal nerve exam).

A contradiction means two parts of the record say opposite things about the same fact. For example, if the note said "temperature 101" but the vital signs recorded at the same visit said 98.6, the structured record would contradict the note.

**A step-by-step check for C1:**

1. Read the whole record first, every note paragraph included. Look for anything on the list.
2. If you find nothing on the list, C1 is **unsupported**. Evidence can stay empty, because the status rests on what the record does not contain. If a particular exam is what settled it for you (for example, one showing that a leg finding falls short), you may tick it instead. Either is fine.
3. For a nerve finding, check it against the reading above. Leg pain or a positive straight leg raise alone does not count.
4. For a red flag that is on the list, find where it is written. In a structured entry, and not contradicted: **supported**, and tick that entry.
5. Only in the note, or contradicted by a structured entry: **undeterminable**. Tick the note paragraph, and the contradicting entry if there is one.

**Words that carry a condition.** Some items have a qualifier: "unexplained" weight loss, "potentially osteoporotic", "recent" bacterial infection, "clinically significant" or "progressive". Read them literally and check whether the record says enough to meet the words. If the record truly does not let you decide, that is what undeterminable is for.

### C2. Four weeks of conservative care that hasn't worked

The criteria: for a non-red-flag condition, the patient "has not responded to a reasonable trial of conservative management lasting at least four weeks," and the MRI is being considered after at least one month of symptoms.

So C2 has three parts, and all three have to hold:

1. **Conservative care for at least four weeks.** In plain words, conservative care is treatment short of surgery or injections, such as physical therapy or pain medicine. It has to be in a structured entry: a therapy in Procedures, or a medication order in Medications.
2. **No response.** The latest visit or the note says the pain persists, is unchanged, or is worse.
3. **At least a month of symptoms** before the MRI request. Compare the low back pain Condition's onset date with the request date at the top of the page.

**How to count four weeks.** Use the dates printed on the entry: a Procedure's "2026-06-15 to 2026-08-03", or a medication's "dosage period". Four weeks is 28 days. For example, June 1 to June 29 is exactly four weeks.

**Note-only care.** Conservative care stated only in the note, with nothing structured behind it (no therapy Procedure or medication order), or contradicted by the structured record, is labeled undeterminable. Unlike C1, the criteria do not say this alone routes the case; the routing questions in Part 4 decide that.

C2 always gets its own status, even when C1 is supported. The criteria say the criterion an approval does not rest on "is still labeled with its status."

### C3. The MRI will change what happens next

The criteria: "a surgical intervention or other aggressive treatment (for example intervertebral joint injection) is under consideration, and the findings would affect treatment choices."

Two parts:

1. **Surgery or another aggressive treatment is under consideration.** The criteria's example is an intervertebral joint injection. Look at the latest visit and the note's assessment and plan, and read the policy's own wording in the **Policy and criteria** panel.
2. **The MRI's result would affect the treatment choice.** The record should say, in some form, how the result would change what happens next.

The note counts as evidence for C3 on its own.

### C4. Not a repeat of earlier imaging

The criteria: "The study is not a duplication of other imaging (such as a spinal CT) unless documentation supports the need for both, for example inconclusive findings on the prior study."

Look for earlier imaging of the lumbar spine in Procedures, Imaging reports, and the note.

- **No earlier lumbar imaging anywhere:** supported, with **empty** evidence. The status rests on what the record does not contain.
- **Earlier lumbar imaging exists:** ask whether the MRI would duplicate it (the criteria's example is a spinal CT). If it would, check whether the record documents the need for both, such as an inconclusive result on the earlier study, and label from what you find.

The note counts as evidence for C4 on its own.

## Part 4. Routing the case

After the four criteria, choose one outcome. Ask these questions in order and stop at the first "yes":

1. **Is there a red flag stated only in the note, or one the structured record contradicts?** Route. (The C1 rule.)
2. **Are C3 and C4 supported, and C1 or C2 supported with cited evidence?** Approve. A case needs a red flag or a failed four-week trial, not both. (C4's evidence can be empty when there is no earlier imaging; that still counts as supported.)
3. **Otherwise the case is not approvable. Does the record contradict itself on any criterion?** Route, even if a document could resolve it.
4. **Would one record item that the provider could send settle the open question?** Request, and name the criterion and the missing item. If no single document could make the case approvable, route.

These four questions restate the criteria's own sentences: "A case is approvable when C3 and C4 hold and either C1 or C2 holds with cited evidence," and, when a case is not approvable, "request when one record element the provider could send would settle the question, naming the criterion and the element; route when the record contradicts itself on a criterion, even if a document could resolve it, or when the case could not be approved whatever such a document said."

**Filling in a request.** Choose **request**. Two boxes appear. In the first, pick the criterion the gap belongs to. In the second, type what is missing in a few plain words, then click anywhere outside the box so it saves.

## Part 5. Habits for the 12 cases

- **Read the whole record before answering anything.** C1 in particular depends on every part of it.
- **For each status, ask "what in the record shows this?"** Then tick those ids.
- **Approve only when C3 and C4 hold and you can point to the evidence** for whichever of C1 or C2 the approval rests on. That is the approve rule itself.
- **When the record truly doesn't let you decide,** use undeterminable. It is a real answer, not a failure.
- **Keep a short paper list** of case numbers that felt hard and why, in a sentence. Those are the first ones to look at when disagreements are settled.
- **Pace:** about 12 minutes a case, three or four cases a sitting.
- **You are not the last word.** Your labels, the third reader's, and the locked intended answers are compared, and every disagreement is settled against the policy text. Hard calls are expected, and they are what the settling step is for.
